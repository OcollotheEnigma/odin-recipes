Githeri

Description

Githeri is one of Kenya's most enduring upcountry dishes, originating from the Agikuyu (Kikuyu) people of the fertile Central Highlands around Mount Kenya. It is a humble, hearty one-pot meal of boiled maize and red kidney beans, cooked low and slow until both are tender, then enriched with a fried tomato and onion base.

Githeri became famous across the country as a school meal — generations of Kenyan students grew up eating it from aluminium sufurias in boarding school dining halls. But do not let its simplicity fool you. A well-made githeri, seasoned with cumin and fresh tomatoes and served with a side of avocado, is deeply satisfying and nutritious. In 2017 it briefly went viral worldwide when a Kenyan man was photographed calmly eating githeri from a plastic bag while waiting to vote — an image that captured the quiet dignity of the dish perfectly.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dry maize kernels (dried corn), soaked overnight
  • 1 cup dry red kidney beans, soaked overnight
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 2 medium potatoes, cubed (optional but traditional)
  • Fresh coriander or sukuma wiki (kale) to serve alongside

Steps

  1. Drain the soaked maize and beans. Place them together in a large pot and cover with fresh water by about 5cm above the level of the maize.
  2. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to medium-low. Cook for 45 minutes to 1 hour until both the maize and beans are fully tender. Add more water if needed to keep them submerged. Drain and set aside.
  3. If using potatoes, boil them separately until just tender, then drain.
  4. In a separate pan, heat the oil over medium heat. Add the onion and fry until soft and translucent, about 5 minutes.
  5. Add garlic and cook for one minute. Then add the chopped tomatoes, cumin, paprika, salt, and pepper.
  6. Cook the tomato mixture for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it reduces into a thick, fragrant sauce.
  7. Add the cooked maize and beans to the pan. Add the potatoes if using. Stir everything together to coat well in the sauce.
  8. Cook on low heat for a further 5 minutes so the flavours merge.
  9. Taste and adjust seasoning. Serve hot with chapati, ugali, or as a standalone meal with fresh avocado on the side.
