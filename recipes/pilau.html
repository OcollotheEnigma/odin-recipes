Pilau

Description

Pilau is one of the most celebrated dishes of Kenya's Swahili coast. It is a richly spiced rice dish with deep Arab and Indian roots, brought to the Kenyan coast centuries ago by traders who sailed the Indian Ocean. The word itself comes from the Persian "pilav," and the dish has been lovingly adapted into a Kenyan staple ever since.

What sets Kenyan pilau apart is the pilau masala, a warm blend of cardamom, cumin, cloves, cinnamon, and black pepper that perfumes the entire pot. It is traditionally served at weddings, family celebrations, and Friday meals after Jumu'ah prayers in coastal towns like Mombasa, Malindi, and Lamu.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups pishori or basmati rice, washed and soaked for 30 minutes
  • 500g beef or goat meat, cut into chunks
  • 1 large onion, finely sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 4 cups beef or chicken stock
  • 2 tsp pilau masala (or: 1 tsp cumin, 4 cardamom pods, 4 cloves, 1 cinnamon stick, 1 tsp black pepper)
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • Salt to taste
  • Fresh coriander leaves for garnish

Steps

  1. Heat oil in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and fry until deep golden brown. This step is essential — the caramelised onion gives pilau its signature colour and flavour.
  2. Add the whole spices (cardamom, cloves, cinnamon stick) and fry for one minute until fragrant.
  3. Add garlic and ginger paste. Stir and cook for two minutes.
  4. Add the meat and brown on all sides, about 8 minutes.
  5. Add the chopped tomatoes, cumin, black pepper, and salt. Cook until the tomatoes break down completely, about 10 minutes.
  6. Pour in the stock and bring to a boil. Reduce heat, cover, and simmer for 20 minutes until the meat is tender.
  7. Drain the soaked rice and add it to the pot. Stir gently to combine with the meat and stock.
  8. Bring back to a gentle boil, then reduce heat to very low. Cover tightly with foil and then the lid.
  9. Cook undisturbed for 20 to 25 minutes until all the liquid is absorbed and the rice is fluffy.
  10. Remove the lid, fluff with a fork, and garnish with fresh coriander. Serve hot.
